# Presenting Kessel

Open http://localhost:8080 in Chrome on the M1. Use a laptop viewport around
1440 x 900, allow Earth to appear, and keep the concept caption visible. All
rendering assets are local; the Sources links are optional external references.

## Main route: about 60 seconds

| Time | Action | Point to convey |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0–6 s | View Earth, then Start experience | Extreme environments are the longer-term scope. |
| 6–14 s | Browse to Underground | Underground is the first deployment environment. |
| 14–21 s | Explore underground | A continuous journey reaches Rwanda. |
| 21–27 s | View surface, then Enter the mine | This is an authored mine at a real location. |
| 27–41 s | View the cutaway and queued trucks | Weak connectivity and inefficient haulage. |
| 41–45 s | View the trained site | Switch to an illustrative trained-policy comparison. |
| 45–55 s | Observe the second haul loop and figures | The same batch completes sooner, with lower cost. |
| 55–60 s | Discover Kessel | Normal scrolling introduces the proposed product. |

Timing is a presentation cue, not forced auto-advance. Skip flight is available
for repeated presentations. The main mine stays overhead. Before and Trained
site can be compared repeatedly.

## Optional technical branch

Explore the world model from either the mine or product. The eight-second
sequence represents 2.4 million authored episodes. It is not live training.
Observation geometry, predicted poses, parallel rollout silhouettes, actions,
reward and policy checkpoints illustrate reinforcement learning. Early misses
the pile, Learning collects a partial load and spills, and Trained scoops,
hauls and tips ore into the delivery bay. Each checkpoint can replay on its
own. Pause freezes the scene and counter. Replay training restarts the sequence.

Return to mine/product or Escape restores the comparison and page position.
The mine clock is paused while the optional view is open.

## Product

Scroll through learned dynamics, reinforcement learning, policy distillation
and offline onboard control. Deployment data returns when connected, feeding
future world-model updates. The exploded kit is proposed hardware. Focus its
seven component labels to highlight them. Assemble the kit brings it together;
the same control separates it again. Replay experience starts at Earth.

## Figures

Four trucks move a 704 t batch. Baseline throughput is 88 t/h; trained throughput
is 132 t/h. The authored assumptions imply 8 h versus 5 h 20 min, operating cost
£12.95/t versus £8.03/t, £3,467 saved and 2 h 40 min saved for the same batch.
These figures are illustrative, not customer results. Weak connectivity stays
weak: the product proposal is local autonomy, not a better network.
